How it works

It watches the book, not the chart.

Every second, for every market you've enabled, it pulls the full order book and measures where the resting size actually sits.

Reads

The live order book

Depth on both sides, the spread, and how fair value has moved over the last few seconds — the things that move before the price does.

Filters

Most windows, out

Wide spreads, thin books, the first and last minutes of a window, and setups where the signal components disagree are all skipped. Doing nothing is the common case.

Executes

Entry through exit

A sell order goes in the moment an entry fills. If neither side fills, the window settles and the result is booked — net of fees, both ways.

Kalshi charges a fee on entry and exit — around 4¢ of round-trip movement near mid-book. Exit targets below that floor are raised automatically rather than booked as losing “wins”.

Paper trades the real book

It simulates fills against production liquidity, not the demo book — so what you measure in paper is measured against depth that genuinely exists. That is the only reason paper results mean anything.

Demo proves the plumbing, not the edge

Signing, fills, partial fills, rejects, rate limits — all real. But the demo book is thin, so its profit and loss is not evidence about the strategy and is never presented as though it were.

Read this  /  before you pay for anything

No edge is being claimed here.

The bundled strategies are reference implementations of ordinary microstructure reasoning — book imbalance, short-horizon drift, spread and liquidity filters. They have not been shown to be profitable, and this page will not show you a win rate, an equity curve, or a screenshot of a good day.

A high win rate is not profit. It is entirely possible to win three trades in four and still lose money, because the fee is charged twice and the losses are full-stake. That is the single easiest way to mistake a losing system for a winning one, and it is why the numbers you would normally see on a page like this are absent from it.

What ships instead is the apparatus for finding out: a recorder that captures the live book to disk, a replay engine that scores a strategy net of real fees, and an analysis tool that refuses to report a verdict until the sample is large enough to support one. Run it in paper mode, record for a couple of weeks, and let the data decide.

Questions

Good to know before you start.

Whose account does it trade on?

Your own. You create an API key in your Kalshi account and connect it in the bot. Your funds never leave your account — the bot only places orders through your key, and it cannot withdraw or move money. Remove the key any time with /disconnect.

How much control do I have over how it trades?

Full control. You choose the coins, the strategy, how many contracts per signal, the price band you're willing to enter at, your exit rule, and your risk caps — daily loss limit, maximum open exposure, a balance floor it won't spend below, and how many trades it can take per window. You can stop it at any moment with /stop, or stop everything with /kill.

Do I need to trade real money?

Not to start, and you shouldn't. Paper mode runs the identical code path against the real production order book and simulates every fill, so you can watch it trade for as long as you like without a Kalshi account at all. Every new account starts there.

Where does it run?

Wherever you put it. It runs on your own machine — Windows, macOS or Linux — or on any small server or container host you prefer. There is a one-command setup script for each. It trades while it is running and does nothing while it is not, so if you want it going around the clock, run it somewhere that stays awake.

Is this guaranteed to make money?

No, and anyone who tells you otherwise is lying. Trading involves a real risk of loss, losing windows happen, and past or backtested results do not guarantee future results. This is a tool for executing a strategy consistently and measuring it honestly — it is not a promise of profit. Only trade money you can afford to lose.

Has the strategy been proven profitable?

No. That is the plain answer, and it is why this page shows no performance figures. What ships is the machinery to find out for yourself: a recorder, a replay engine that scores net of real Kalshi fees, and a signal analysis tool that withholds any verdict until the sample is genuinely large enough. Roughly two weeks of recording is what it takes to resolve the question either way.

Why sell access instead of just trading it yourself?

Position size on 15-minute markets is capped by the liquidity in each window — past a certain size you move the price against yourself and any edge shrinks. Access fees are revenue that doesn't compete with your own fills, and they fund the infrastructure and the ongoing research.

How do I pay and get access?

Pick a plan above and it opens the bot on that tier. Pay in BTC, ETH or USDT to the address it shows you, and once the payment is confirmed your access key is delivered in the chat. Redeem it with /redeem and you're in.

What happens if it crashes mid-order?

Every order is written to disk before it is sent, keyed by the ID it will carry. On the next start, anything with no recorded outcome is checked against Kalshi directly, so an order that reached the exchange while the process was down is found and booked rather than lost.
