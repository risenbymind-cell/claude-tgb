Nothing yet. Most windows produce no setup — that is the filters working.

Venue says which is which. A paper row was filled by the simulator: entries lift the recorded ask capped by resting size, exits fill only when the bid actually reaches the target. A live row was filled by Kalshi — its CLID is the real exchange order ID, and its entry price and fee are what the exchange reported, not estimates.

Open positions are flattened 45s before expiry rather than carried into a settlement. P&L is net of Kalshi's fee on both legs. A live position whose exit is refused stays open and is marked "exit failed" — it is not written off, because the contracts are still on the exchange until it says otherwise.

No edge has been demonstrated for any strategy here. Read the net column, not the win rate — small targets can win nine times in ten and still lose money, because the fee is charged twice.

Circuit

Shadow lets the selected strategy fire and log signals exactly as normal, without opening a position — for watching what it would do before letting it act. Reconcile compares the positions this desk thinks it holds against what Kalshi actually reports, and names every divergence; in paper mode there is nothing to compare, and it says so. Check clock compares this machine's clock to Kalshi's, the same check the bot runs at boot.

Production arming

Arming is required before this desk will send a production order, and it is cleared on every restart. It cannot widen the backend's gate: real orders also require the process itself to have been started with TRADING_MODE=production-live and ALLOW_PRODUCTION_ORDERS set, and step 2 below refuses outright when it was not. Demo-live needs no arming — demo funds are not money, and a ceremony there would only train you to click through the one that guards real money.

Can this data answer anything yet?

Every statistical tool here refuses to return a verdict on a sample that is too small — which is the most valuable thing they do, and also invisible: you run the command, it says NOT ENOUGH DATA, and nothing tells you how far off you are. This is that distance, as a number.

Why 200. Below roughly that many settled windows, a search over thousands of strategy plans will always find something that looks excellent, because with enough candidates and few enough observations one of them fits the noise. The threshold is not caution — it is the point at which a result starts to carry information.

Balance matters as much as count. A full sample where 90% of markets settled the same way is one directional move counted many times, not many independent observations. The tooling withholds a verdict on that too, and says so here rather than reporting a confident number that is really a measurement of one trend.

Connect Kalshi keys

Checking MASTER_KEY strength…

Nothing to connect here. This instance runs as a sandbox: it reads Kalshi's public market data, simulates every fill, and has no order path to any exchange. Run it yourself without --sandbox to connect your own keys.

The key ID and private key are sent to this desk process only — the same process already holding your .env. They are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM (via Fernet, keyed by your MASTER_KEY) before being written to disk, and the private key is never echoed back — only a masked ID and a fingerprint. This connects the desk's own market-data/order client; it does not by itself enable production orders (see the Risk tab).

Runbooks

Static reference, not live data — procedures for the situations this desk is built to handle.

Stop trading immediately

  1. Click Kill on the Desk or Risk tab, or run /kill from the Telegram bot if it is running.
  2. The kill switch is also a file at <data dir>/KILL — creating it by hand from a shell works even if this page is unreachable.
  3. Confirm the header chip reads KILLED. The strategy loop keeps evaluating and logging decisions while killed; it only stops opening new positions.
  4. Click Resume (or delete the KILL file) when ready to continue.

Understand a mode

  • Paper — no orders sent anywhere; simulated fills against the real production book.
  • Demo-live — real orders on Kalshi's demo exchange, spending demo funds. The demo book is thin, so P/L there is not evidence about the strategy, only about whether the order code works.
  • Production-live — real orders, real money. Requires two independent environment variables at process start; this desk cannot change your money's exposure by itself.

Recorder went quiet

  1. Check the Markets tab — zero rows with markets known to be open on Kalshi means the feed, not the exchange, is the problem.
  2. Click Reconcile on the Risk tab to force a fresh discovery pass.
  3. If that does not recover it, restart the desk process — a pooled connection dying under a suspended container is indistinguishable from an empty result otherwise.

Before arming production

  • Read the Ledger tab's own disclaimer: no edge has been demonstrated for any strategy here.
  • Confirm MASTER_KEY is a real generated key, not the auto-generated ephemeral one — check the warning banner on the Connect tab.
  • Run a clock check on the Risk tab; signed requests fail silently as auth errors when the clock has drifted, which reads like a bad key.
